📖 About this page

🎓 The Project

Domestic Network Analysis (SCON 2020) scans the WiFi spectrum around a home with a monitor-mode adapter (wifi_scanner.py, scapy) and analyses the collected beacons: signal strength per room, channel occupancy of the 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz bands, and two simulations — optimal router placement and repeater placement — based on a simple path-loss model fitted from the measurements.

📶 The model

Predicted signal in a room: dBm = dBm_max − (distance_m × 3 dB/m + walls × 7 dB/wall) where the wall count between zones comes from a hand-built 10×10 matrix (estimated from the scan) and distances are scaled to metres. The score of a placement is the zone-weighted mean linear power (living areas weighted 1.5×, bedrooms 1.25×).

🗺️ Flat plans

Four floor plans: the real scanned home (rebuilt as a clean tiled plan) plus three designed flats — Estudio, Piso 2 hab and Loft. The map is drawn as a discrete subdivision grid: use the slider to choose how big the cells are, and every cell predicts its own signal (distance to the router + wall attenuation), so the heatmap is smooth and walls show real drops.

🧪 Simulate

Drag the router anywhere on the floor plan — every room's predicted dBm and the score list update live. Toggle the repeater to add a second source (drag the repeater too; each room takes the best of the two signals). Adjust the model's dB-per-metre and dB-per-wall sliders to see how the optimum moves. ✨ Find optimal room runs the notebook's exhaustive search (every room as the source) and highlights the winner.

🖥️ Implementation

The model functions from the notebook were extracted into analysis_model.py (unit-tested) and ported 1:1 to JavaScript for this page. Source: github.com/alejp1998/domestic_net_analysis.
